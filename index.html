Monohybrid Cross Lab

Practise Mendel's crosses: parents, gametes, Punnett squares, F1 and F2.

Test mode

Work through a mixed set of questions like a summative. Every box is blank, there are no hints and nothing is checked until you hand in. Move between questions as you like, then submit to see your mark and review the working for each question. Each correct answer is worth 1 mark.

Key terms and the five steps
Allele
One version of a gene, written as a letter. A capital is dominant, a small letter is recessive.
Genotype
The pair of alleles an organism has, such as Tt.
Phenotype
The trait you can see, such as tall or dwarf.
Homozygous
Two identical alleles (TT or tt).
Heterozygous
Two different alleles (Tt).
Gamete
A sex cell. It carries only one allele from each pair.
F1 and F2
The first and second generations after the parents (P). F2 comes from crossing two F1 organisms.
Incomplete dominance
Neither allele fully hides the other, so the heterozygote is in between (a red plant crossed with a white plant gives pink offspring).
Codominance
Both alleles are fully expressed in the heterozygote (a roan cow has red and white hairs; blood group AB makes both A and B antigens).
Multiple alleles
A gene with more than two versions in the population. ABO blood groups use three alleles: IA, IB and i. Each person has only two of them.
Test cross
Crossing an organism showing the dominant trait with a homozygous recessive one, to find out whether it is TT or Tt.
Method
1. Parent genotypes. 2. Gametes. 3. Punnett square. 4. Genotype ratio. 5. Phenotype ratio and probability.
